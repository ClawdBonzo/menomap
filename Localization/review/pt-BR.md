# pt-BR: review

Strong, natural Brazilian Portuguese: consistent você with no tu/te slips, Ministério da Saúde patient terms (fogacho with 'ondas de calor' where explanatory, suores noturnos, terapia hormonal with TRH/TH, sangramento após a menopausa, which avoids the climatério trap), Apple pt-BR platform terms (app Saúde, Tela Bloqueada, Central de Controle, Toque Duplo, Compartilhamento Familiar, figurinhas), and safety strings that are faithful and complete, including 'mesmo uma única vez, mesmo leve'. The wry lines land warmly ('Que desaforo.', 'tô derretendo', 'Casaquinho: tira, põe, tira de novo', 'Controle do ar: meu'), and 'Plantão Noturno' reads naturally as 'on duty for you tonight', not as a confusing hospital shift, so it stays. Changes are small: times placed after 'às/das' (wrong at 1 o'clock, e.g. 'às 01:12') recast as labels, a Siri hint that quoted an unlocalized phrase, the 'Tell my person' calque ('minha pessoa' reads as 'myself' in Brazil), a brain-fog anchor that read as 'light fog', one clunky double 'para', a calque in the store description and one more keyword.

## Residual doubts

- 'Plantão Noturno' was kept: Brazilians hear 'de plantão' as 'on call / standing by for you', which suits the feature; the hospital-shift association is mild and not confusing in context. 'Vigília Noturna' would be the alternative, but it sounds religious.
- #819 'quando era %2$@' (weekday name) is grammatical but a little stiff; left because every alternative runs into weekday gender/plural agreement (segunda-feira f., sábado/domingo m.).
- Energy anchors 'Vazio'/'Cheio' kept; 'Empty' is shared with other contexts, so changing it risks mismatches elsewhere.
