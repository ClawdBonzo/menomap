# id: review

A careful, warm and medically sound Indonesian translation. Patient terms match Kemenkes/major-hospital usage (keringat malam, menopause, perimenopause, pascamenopause, terapi hormon, perdarahan setelah menopause), every safety string is faithful, bleeding strings clearly mean after periods have stopped ("setelah menopause" / "12 bulan tidak haid" is read in Indonesian as after haid has ended, not after the transition years), placeholders and plurals are intact, Apple terms follow support.apple.com/id-id, and no Malaysian-Malay words slipped in. The "kamu" register is held consistently and is the norm for Indonesian consumer health apps (Halodoc and similar), so I kept it. Fixes are small: two safety-string precision points ("secara langsung" was ambiguous between in-person and immediately; "biasanya" strengthened "often"), a calque in two articles ("kekeringan", "kafein yang terlambat"), HRT glossed as terapi hormon in UI where Indonesian users are less familiar with the English acronym, one wry line whose word "penyintas" carries disaster/cancer weight, a non-standard verb form, and a few awkward phrasings.

## Residual doubts

- Form of address: kept "kamu". Indonesian consumer health apps (Halodoc, most telehealth and lifestyle apps) use kamu; Apple, banks and Alodokter articles use Anda. For 40-60-year-old women Anda would be the more deferential choice, but I am not confident it is expected in an app, and the translation holds kamu consistently. If Rob wants the more formal tone, it must be a full pass (kamu/-mu -> Anda everywhere, keep aku for the user's own voice and saya in the PDF questions).
- Hot flash: "rasa panas mendadak" is clear and used by hospitals, but Indonesian patient sites (Kemenkes Ayo Sehat, Alodokter, Halodoc) very often keep the English "hot flashes". The article (id 162) glosses it once; the store keywords include hot,flash. Subtitle "Rasa Panas & Catatan Dokter" could alternatively be "Hot Flash & Catatan Dokter" for search; left as is.
- "Selamat siang" for Good afternoon (id 264): Indonesian splits siang (~11-15) and sore (~15-18); siang is acceptable for the whole afternoon slot.
